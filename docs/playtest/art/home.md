# Art & feel playtest: home (farm + Emberhollow village)

Persona: a cozy-game art and feel critic and Stardew Valley fan. New game ("Wren", ponytail, green shirt), played Mon 1 06:00 to Tue 2 10:00 Spring. I tilled, planted and watered, chopped a tree, scythed, mined a stone and fished at the pond. In the village I visited Mara's store, the stables, the museum, the tavern (by day and at 21:00), the smithy and the carpentry. I watched the evening and night in the plaza, then slept in the tent.
Screenshots are in the harness scratchpad (`a3_<n>.png`).

## Summary: 6/10 for cozy and alive

The world art is good and consistent. Ground, trees, buildings, interiors and the animal icons all read as one hand-pixelled style, and several frames look as good as Stardew (the tavern facade, the village at night with lit windows, the shop and smithy interiors). The weak part is the life. The plaza is a huge, mostly empty cobble rectangle, villagers are few and scattered, the tavern has no patrons in the evening, and the farm starts as a bare meadow with a tiny tent. Lovely buildings, but nobody living in them.

## Populated?

**Village by time of day**
- **8:00** (a3_16, a3_17): 2 villagers in the plaza (Mara, Brann). The plaza is very large (map a3_28: about 60% of the village is one cobble rectangle).
- **9:00-13:00** (a3_23, a3_40): the plaza is empty. At 12:30 there were zero people on screen in the middle of the square (a3_40). Shopkeepers are behind their counters, which is right, but the streets are dead.
- **17:30-19:40** (a3_55, a3_57, a3_58): the best moment. 4-5 villagers walk the plaza and the paths to the tavern, and the lanterns light up. This is when it feels like a village.
- **21:00** (a3_61, a3_62): two villagers stand outside the tavern facing the wall beside the door (x≈750-800, door at 860), as if stuck queueing. Inside, the Drowsy Drake has only Pip behind the bar and one empty table. Stardew's saloon is the evening hub; here nobody is inside.
- No animals, pets, birds or children walk the village. There are no benches, flower beds, market crates or laundry: the plaza holds a well, a fountain, an altar with purple orbs, a notice board and 6 lamp posts on bare cobbles.
- Chat with Mara and Elias kept showing the same greeting line (a3_21, a3_59). From the player's side, Chat seems to do nothing.

**Farm**
- On day 1 the farm is a big flat meadow with evenly sprinkled stones, twigs and weed tufts (a3_6, a3_49). The debris is spread too evenly, so it reads as noise rather than "overgrown grandmother's farm". There are no clusters, no big logs or boulders, and no fallen fence or ruined foundation of the old house.
- The tent is small and plain (a3_6): a two-tone triangle with no pegs, ropes, bedroll or lantern. Next to the huge greenhouse ruin it looks like a placeholder. The campfire is tiny and nearly invisible at night (a3_64): no glow, no circle of stones you can read.
- The leather "chest" south-east of the campfire (a3_6) is unlabelled and its purpose isn't clear.
- The pond (a3_50) is a blocky square-ish blob. The water texture's tiling is visible as a repeating darker diamond pattern.
- The stray pet is hinted at (a brown shape at the screen edge in a3_49) but it isn't there on day 1.

## Art consistency and beauty

**Lovely**
- The Drowsy Drake exterior (a3_31): timber upper floor, dormers, flower boxes, mug sign. Also the stables' board-and-batten walls and the museum's portico and urn sign (a3_37). These are Stardew-quality.
- Night in the village (a3_61, a3_63): warm lit windows on dark facades. This is the most beautiful view in the session.
- Interiors (a3_24 store, a3_42 tavern, a3_74 smithy, a3_76 carpentry): coherent palette, warm wood, good props (forge glow, weapon rack, saddles on stands, kegs, bottle shelves, banners, paintings).
- Trees and conifers in the tree lines, tall grass, the fountain and the well.
- Odile's animal icons (a3_35) are charming and clear.
- The character's back view with the ponytail (a3_19) and the fishing pose with line and rod (a3_51).

**Off-style or weak**
1. **The cobble plaza texture** (a3_16, a3_40) is very busy: high-contrast cells with green moss specks repeated everywhere. On a field this big it becomes visual static and fights the characters for readability. It turns muddy brown under the sunset grade (a3_58).
2. **Roofs of mixed fidelity.** The thatch and shingle roofs are noisy vertical striping (cottage a3_18, a3_75). The smithy's slate roof is almost flat dark blue with barely any texture (a3_17). The navy slate on the manor and museum is fine.
3. **The greenhouse ruin** (a3_6, a3_64): the broken panes are a checkerboard of black squares, which reads as missing texture rather than broken glass. At night it renders as a saturated electric-blue block (a3_64), which is the most out-of-place thing on the farm.
4. **The tree-line walls** (a3_37, a3_75) are the same oak and pine alternating in a perfect row, like a fence of clones. They need some jitter in size, spacing and species, plus a few bushes.
5. **Dialogue portraits** are tiny full-body sprites (a3_20, a3_59) instead of a face portrait. Stardew lives on expressive portraits, and this is the single biggest cozy gap in the UI.
6. **Character creator** (a3_3, a3_4): in side view the mouth or nose pixel is red and reads like a bleeding lip. The ponytail isn't visible from the side. The preview sits in a huge, mostly empty window.
7. **Soil**: tilled tiles are hard squares with no edge autotiling (a3_46). Seedlings are tiny 6px blobs.
8. **Canopy fade ghosts** (a3_9, a3_11, a3_37, a3_50): a semi-transparent tree is sometimes drawn when the player isn't really behind it. It looks like a rendering glitch rather than a feature.
9. **A dirt patch in the plaza** (a3_16, a3_27): a lone sand or dirt splotch on the cobbles west of the fountain looks like a mistake.
10. **A stray grey pixel or particle** floats in the black void outside rooms (store a3_24 at about 503,145; museum a3_38 at about 1207,370; tavern a3_42). It is probably an ambient mote that isn't clipped to the room.
11. **Carpentry wood pile** pokes past the right wall into the void (a3_76, x 1112→1165).
12. **Trade signs over windows**: Hale's carpentry sign hangs on top of a window (a3_63, a3_75).
13. **Museum floor** is grey dirt with green specks, as if grass grows indoors (a3_38). Room windows show daylight at night (a3_62).
14. **Bobber on sand**: I clicked deep water, but the bobber landed on the sand south of me (a3_51).

**Scale**
- Characters, doors and furniture agree, and doors fit a person.
- The tent is undersized next to the greenhouse.
- The museum and tavern rooms are small next to their facades: the "museum" is one small room with 4 urns.

## UI look

- The parchment-and-wood frames, the dock icons and the hotbar are consistent and clean (a3_6).
- Windows are often huge with little in them: the letter (a3_5), the morning report (a3_66) and the creator all fill 90% of the screen with one short block of text. Smaller, centred cards would feel cozier.
- The shop list (a3_25) is a full-width row of text with 16px icons. Stardew's shop has a portrait, bigger icons and a compact grid.
- The "Click ×5 again to buy" confirm pops up right where the next click lands. Two quick clicks bought more than I meant and dropped me to 97g (a3_26).
- Hover labels ("Potato · day 0/6 · watered", a3_46) are big white outlined text that covers the crops.
- The camera clamps at map edges, so the player ends up under the HUD dock or hotbar (a3_54, a3_37). The hotbar covers the museum door (a3_36).
- The satchel tab bar wraps onto two rows of 13 tabs, which looks busy.

## Atmosphere

- **Day and night**: the dawn pink (a3_67), golden hour (a3_57) and night (a3_61) are all distinct. The sunset grade (a3_58) is heavy: everything goes red-brown and the grass turns olive. Night is a flat purple-grey, so lanterns and the campfire are tiny lit squares with no presence. Even within the "no light map" rule, a small flat pixel halo ring around lanterns, fires and windows would help a lot.
- **Chimney smoke** is barely visible (a few grey squares, a3_17). **Particles**: some petals.
- **Sound**: not assessable through the harness.
- **Weather**: clear both days.
- **Time pace**: about 10 game minutes every 6 s. The day felt long enough to enjoy.

## Concrete ideas (ranked)

1. **Fill the evening tavern.** Make the Drowsy Drake the 18:00-24:00 hub: 3-6 villagers seated at tables and stools, plus more tables. Fix the queue at the tavern wall.
2. **Face portraits** in the talk window (a 64x64 bust per villager, with 2-3 expressions), and make Chat show a new line every time.
3. **Shrink and dress the plaza.** Cut the cobble area by about 40% and add grass islands, flower beds, benches, crates, a market stall, a tree in a planter and bunting. Calm the cobble texture: lower contrast, fewer moss specks, larger stones.
4. **Daytime street life**: villagers stroll, sit on benches and chat in pairs between their shop shifts, and there are a few ambient animals (cats, chickens near a cottage, birds that fly off).
5. **A better farm start**: a characterful tent (ropes, bedroll, lantern, cooking pot), a ruined house foundation, debris in clumps, and a few large logs or boulders as early goals. Give the campfire a visible stone ring and a night glow.
6. **Greenhouse ruin**: draw real broken glass (shards, cracked frames, vines through gaps) instead of a black checkerboard, and fix its night colour.
7. **Break up the tree lines** with varied spacing, sizes, bushes and the odd birch.
8. **Lantern, window and fire halos**: flat stepped rings drawn after the grade, so nights feel warm.
9. **Roof textures**: tone down the thatch and shingle noise, and give the smithy slate some courses.
10. **Soil autotiling** and bigger seedlings.

## Top 5 fixes

1. The evening tavern is empty, and villagers get stuck facing the tavern wall at 21:00 (a3_61, a3_62).
2. The plaza is huge, empty and noisy: shrink it, decorate it, calm the texture (a3_40).
3. The talk window has no face portrait, and Chat repeats the same line (a3_20, a3_59).
4. The greenhouse ruin is a checkerboard by day and electric blue at night (a3_6, a3_64).
5. Small rendering glitches: stray void pixel in rooms, carpentry wood pile through the wall, canopy-fade ghost trees, plaza dirt patch, the red mouth pixel in the creator, bobber landing on sand (a3_24, a3_76, a3_9, a3_16, a3_4, a3_51).
